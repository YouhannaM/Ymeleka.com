What I work on

Atoms, machines
and software

I work across the AI supply chain, from the silicon to the decision a machine makes in the field. Every layer has to arrive on time, in spec and at cost.

The loop that turns models into machines that work in the real world: specs, suppliers, tests and deployment. I build it for Ficio robots in the orchard, for CUPI robots at Cornell University Physical Intelligence, and for AI-native teams where people and machines work as one system. At Tesla I ran it for closures, electro-mechanical parts and seals, and was recognized by Elon Musk as Top Talent.

Perception, grading and a data layer that learns from every apple. One shared stack, so each new robot is cheaper and faster to build than the last.

Cameras, lights and processors that survive dust, rain and a tractor hitch. A model is only as good as the signal that reaches it.

At Cornell I research wide-bandgap semiconductors in the Jena-Xing Lab: the materials that move power efficiently. I also work on data center architecture projects: power, cooling and compute for AI at scale.

A thesis

We've solved scarcity before

1956 · The box

Ships

A standard steel container made the world's goods cheap to move. Ports, ships and trucks finally spoke one language.

1958 · The jet

Planes

Jets turned an ocean crossing from a week into an evening. Distance stopped being a reason not to go.

1969 · The network

The internet

A handful of connected computers became a way to copy knowledge almost for free, to anyone, anywhere.

Now · The food machine

Food

Food touches every life, yet most of it is still picked, sorted and prepped by hand. Specialized, intelligent hardware can make it abundant.

Every time we built the right machine, something scarce became ordinary

01 · Cairo, Egypt

Cairo

I started my first business at fourteen, importing candy and reselling it to friends. By sixteen I was working in manufacturing.

Every shelf has a supply chain behind it. Cairo is where I first saw it.

→ so I went to study how things are made
02 · Penn State, Pennsylvania

Happy Valley

Industrial, Systems & Manufacturing Engineering. Queues, flows and bottlenecks, the math of moving things well.

On game day, a hundred thousand people move through Beaver Stadium as one system.

→ so I went looking for the hardest factory problem
03 · Tesla, California

California

Global Supply Manager for closures, electro-mechanical parts and seals. Recognized by Elon Musk as Tesla Top Talent.

A car is thousands of decisions that all have to arrive on time.

→ so I went one layer deeper, into the chips
04 · Cornell, New York

Ithaca

MEng in Semiconductors and Quantum Materials, researching wide-bandgap semiconductors in the Jena-Xing Lab. And Ficio: three robots across four hardware iterations in eight weeks, shown to 300 growers at a field day.

The machines are new. The question is the one I asked in Cairo: how does food get to people?
